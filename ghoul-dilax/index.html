The same words, read three ways. Nothing changes between the first two passes except the rhythm.

Running this with a class

  1. Don't say the title. Keep pass three hidden until the end.
  2. To halt a read-through mid-sentence: press Escape, or the Stop button. To pause and pick up where you left off, press the spacebar or tap anywhere outside the buttons.
  3. Play pass one. Ask what they heard. They'll say nothing.
  4. Play pass two. Most students break through within two or three sentences.
  5. Point out that not one letter changed. Prosody did all the work.
  6. Play pass three to confirm, then discuss.

Browser speech synthesis is blunt. If pass two doesn't land, read it aloud yourself — a human reader will always outperform this. Treat the page as a backup or as something students run on their own.

Instructor notes: what the passes actually change

Pass one speaks each word as a separate utterance at low pitch variation with a pause after every word, so the listener gets no clause boundaries and no stress peaks to anchor a parse. Pass two speaks whole clauses with punctuation intact and varied pitch on dialogue lines, restoring the boundary cues. The lexical content is byte-identical.
