Parenting like a Carpenter or a Gardener

Explore how different parenting approaches shape an illustrative career pathway. Make choices across five stages, see how the results change, and explore 115 occupations.

How to play

Your mission: choose a family starting point, guide a child through five stages, and compare the outcome with a career goal.

  1. Set the starting conditions and select a career goal.
  2. At each developmental stage, select Carpenter or Gardener.
  3. See how each choice changes the illustrative outcome.
  4. Review your results, then explore or compare careers in the occupation table.

There is no single correct sequence. Watch the pathway grow beside the decision buttons, then use its stage-by-stage table to compare the same information. Chance plays a part: each stage is a little different every time, and a new run draws a new child from the same family. The page shows the likelihood of reaching the goal before you start and after every stage. Try several rounds with different decisions and compare the results.

Keyboard access: Tab to move between controls, arrow keys to change menu choices, and Enter or Space to select a button.

    How this adapted calculation works

    This page uses the same model as the published Garden or Blueprint activity. Each occupation sits in a three-dimensional space whose axes are difficulty ladders: pay rises a fixed distance for every doubling of income, education a fixed distance for every halving of the share of adults who reach a credential, and emotional labor is scored on a five-dimension rubric. The child starts near the middle of the population, nudged by the family settings and by a random draw, because parents load the dice rather than decide the throw.

    Each Carpenter stage steps toward the chosen occupation, with a little random error in direction and length, and never past it. Each Gardener stage travels further but in a direction the child chooses, which tends to follow what they were already growing into. After five stages the child lands on the best-matched occupation: the nearest one, with larger occupations pulling a little harder and a missing credential counting against a job.

    The likelihood of reaching the goal is measured, not assumed. The page replays the rest of the childhood hundreds of times under every mix of Carpenter and Gardener stages and reports how often the child lands on the goal. The figures use a fixed random seed so the same position always shows the same likelihood.

    These are educational approximations. A match does not establish actual career fit, causal parenting effects, or real-world probabilities of an outcome.
